# Data Transmission

Data transmission is the process of carrying information from one device to another through a communication medium.

At the Physical Layer, information is represented as signals (electrical, optical, or radio) and sent as a stream of bits.

## Basic Concepts

- Data: meaningful information (text, image, audio, video).
- Signal: physical representation of data.
- Sender: device that transmits data.
- Receiver: device that receives data.
- Medium: path between sender and receiver (copper, fiber, air).

## Analog and Digital Signals

### Analog Signal

- Continuous waveform.
- Varies smoothly over time.
- Can take infinitely many values in a range.

Examples:

- traditional voice signals
- AM/FM broadcast waves

### Digital Signal

- Discrete values, usually binary 0 and 1.
- Represented as pulses or voltage levels.
- Easier to regenerate and protect against noise.

Examples:

- Ethernet bit streams
- modern computer and network communication

## Analog and Digital Transmission

- Analog transmission sends analog signals through the medium.
- Digital transmission sends digital signals through the medium.
- Digital data can be sent over analog channels using modulation.
- Analog data can be digitized and sent as digital signals.

## Transmission Direction Modes

### Simplex

- One-way communication only.
- Example: keyboard to CPU, old TV broadcast.

### Half-Duplex

- Two-way communication, but one direction at a time.
- Example: walkie-talkies.

### Full-Duplex

- Two-way communication simultaneously.
- Example: modern switched Ethernet, phone calls.

## Line Configuration

### Point-to-Point

- Dedicated link between exactly two devices.
- Better privacy and predictable performance.
- Example: leased line between two routers.

### Multipoint (Multidrop)

- Multiple devices share one link.
- Lower cost, but shared capacity.
- Example: legacy bus-based systems.

## Data Flow Format

### Parallel Transmission

- Multiple bits sent at the same time on separate lines.
- High speed for short distance.
- Suffers from skew and crosstalk over long distance.

### Serial Transmission

- Bits sent one after another on a single channel.
- Cheaper, simpler, and better for long distance.
- Used in most modern networking.

## Timing and Synchronization

### Asynchronous Transmission

- Data sent byte by byte, each with start and stop bits.
- Simple but has overhead.
- Used in low-speed communication.

### Synchronous Transmission

- Data sent as continuous frames or blocks.
- Sender and receiver share timing.
- More efficient for high-speed communication.

### Isochronous Transmission

- Data delivered at steady time intervals.
- Important for real-time audio/video.
- Focuses on timing consistency over perfect reliability.

## Signal Impairments

During transmission, signals can degrade due to physical effects.

### Attenuation

- Signal loses strength as distance increases.
- Measured in decibels (dB).
- Solved with amplifiers or repeaters.

### Distortion

- Signal shape changes.
- Different frequency components are delayed differently.
- Impacts signal interpretation at receiver.

### Noise

Unwanted signals added to the original signal.

Common noise types:

- thermal noise
- induced electromagnetic noise
- crosstalk
- impulse noise

## Data Rate and Error Concepts

- Bit rate: number of bits transmitted per second (bps).
- Baud rate: number of signal changes (symbols) per second.
- Bit Error Rate (BER): ratio of incorrect bits to total transmitted bits.
- Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR): compares signal power to noise power.

Higher SNR generally means lower BER and better communication quality.

## Encoding and Modulation Overview

### Line Coding (Digital to Digital)

Methods to map bits to signal levels:

- NRZ
- Manchester
- Differential Manchester

Goals:

- clock recovery
- DC balance
- error detection support

### Modulation (Digital to Analog)

When digital data travels over analog carriers:

- ASK (Amplitude Shift Keying)
- FSK (Frequency Shift Keying)
- PSK (Phase Shift Keying)
- QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation)

## Real-World Example

Sending an email from a laptop over Wi-Fi:

1. Data is generated by the application layer.
2. It is converted into frames and bits.
3. Wi-Fi radio modulates bits into electromagnetic waves.
4. Waves travel through air to an access point.
5. Receiver demodulates, checks errors, and rebuilds data.

## Practical Study Notes

- Physical layer is about signals and medium, not logical addressing.
- High speed does not guarantee high quality if BER is high.
- Full-duplex links improve efficiency by removing direction contention.
- Digital transmission dominates because it is robust and scalable.

## Quick Summary

Data transmission is the physical movement of information as signals across media. Understanding signal types, transmission modes, synchronization, and impairments is essential for troubleshooting and designing reliable networks.
