# Network Topology and Its Types

Network topology is the physical or logical arrangement of devices and links in a network.

Two perspectives:

- Physical topology: actual cabling and hardware layout
- Logical topology: how data flows regardless of physical placement

## Why Topology Matters

Topology affects:

- Performance
- Fault tolerance
- Scalability
- Cost
- Troubleshooting complexity

## 1. Bus Topology

Structure:

- All devices share one main communication cable (backbone)

Pros:

- Low cost
- Easy initial setup for small environments

Cons:

- Backbone failure impacts whole network
- Poor scalability
- Hard fault isolation

## 2. Star Topology

Structure:

- All devices connect to a central device (switch/hub)

Pros:

- Easy to add/remove devices
- Better fault isolation
- Common in modern Ethernet LANs

Cons:

- Central device failure can impact all connected nodes
- More cable usage than bus

## 3. Ring Topology

Structure:

- Each device connected to two neighbors, forming a ring

Pros:

- Predictable data path
- Controlled access methods possible

Cons:

- Break in ring can disrupt communication (unless dual ring/redundancy)
- More complex management than star

## 4. Mesh Topology

Structure:

- Devices have many interconnections
- Can be full mesh (every node to every other) or partial mesh

Pros:

- High redundancy and fault tolerance
- Multiple alternate paths

Cons:

- Expensive and complex at scale
- Higher configuration overhead

## 5. Tree Topology

Structure:

- Hierarchical star-like arrangement with parent-child branching

Pros:

- Scalable for larger organizations
- Structured management

Cons:

- Upper-layer device failure affects dependent branches
- More design complexity

## 6. Hybrid Topology

Structure:

- Combination of two or more topologies (for example, star + mesh)

Pros:

- Flexible and optimized for real constraints
- Can balance cost and resilience

Cons:

- Design and troubleshooting can be complex

## Practical Note

Most real enterprise networks are hybrid, not pure single-topology designs.

## Quick Comparison

| Topology | Cost           | Fault Tolerance | Scalability | Complexity     |
| -------- | -------------- | --------------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Bus      | Low            | Low             | Low         | Low            |
| Star     | Medium         | Medium          | High        | Low to medium  |
| Ring     | Medium         | Medium          | Medium      | Medium         |
| Mesh     | High           | Very high       | Medium      | High           |
| Tree     | Medium to high | Medium          | High        | Medium to high |
| Hybrid   | Variable       | Variable        | High        | High           |

## Interview One-Liner

Network topology is the structural design of connections between nodes and links, and it directly impacts performance, resilience, and scalability.
