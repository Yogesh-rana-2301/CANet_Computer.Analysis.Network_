In computer networking, the standard maximum packet size is typically 1,500 bytes (including headers) for Ethernet, known as the Maximum Transmission Unit (MTU). While maximum size is 65,535 bytes, in practice, packets are usually between 20 and 1,500 bytes to avoid fragmentation. Minimum packet size is generally 64 bytes

---

Bandwidth is the theoretical maximum data transfer rate of a network (capacity), while throughput is the actual, measured data rate successfully transmitted at a given time. Think of bandwidth as the total lanes on a highway and throughput as the actual cars passing per minute

---

A MAC address (Media Access Control address) is:
A unique hardware identifier assigned to your device’s network interface (Wi-Fi / Ethernet)
Think of it as:
IP address → temporary, network-based identity
MAC address → permanent, hardware identity

---

Latency is the time delay between a user’s action (request) and the corresponding response, typically measured in milliseconds (ms)

---

A part of a network where two or more devices can send data at the same time and cause a collision

---

Jitter is the variability or inconsistency in latency (ping) over a network, measuring how much packet arrival times fluctuate.
